Growing a chat game on Google Play

A prank chat for fun.

Year
2022
Company
Wee Group
Role
Product Development & Marketing Executive
Team
4 people

A chat game for Android where every level is a conversation and you pick the reply, truth or lie. I joined once it was built, to grow it: new minigame features on the product side, store and ads on the marketing side.

Get the appGoogle Play ↗

01Outcome

Players stay longer in the game, and within a week of launch it ranked top 5 in the USA and top 10 in Canada and Australia for a niche keyword, with ad campaigns that kept the cost per install low.

+50%time per active user (10 to 15 minutes)
100K+downloads, up from 50K+ after the first 3 months
43%of first-time users found it on their own (organic)

02The business problem

The game was ready, but it needed more players, and players who stayed longer, on a tight budget.

Growth usually costs money, so every idea had to earn its place.

03My role

I joined after the game was built, to grow it, covering both product and marketing with my manager and a team of 4: 1 Product Designer, 2 Software Engineers and 1 QC Engineer.

  1. Researched the market

    Studied competitor apps to find what to add next.

  2. Added the minigames

    Designed the minigames between levels and wrote the in-game chat scripts for them, then led engineering to build them.

  3. Ran growth

    App store optimization, creatives, copywriting and ad campaigns.

  4. Watched the numbers

    Tracked revenue and ad costs in reports.

04The solution

Three calls shaped it.

  1. Add minigames between levels that take almost no effort to pass.

    Like scrolling a feed, there's always one more small thing to do, so players stay longer.

  2. Own the chat scripts.

    In a chat game, the script is the gameplay, so it needs one owner.

  3. Test keywords, creatives and ads, round after round.

    Studying competitor apps showed what worked, and trial and error did the rest.

Every level is a chat

Plays on its own. Hover to pause, or pick a step.

05What I learned

This was where I learned the whole product cycle, from build to grow. A feature only counts once people find it and keep playing, so product and marketing had to work as one job.
