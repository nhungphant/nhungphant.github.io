MoMo

A badge system for MoMo's Home screen

A tiny dot with a very huge job.

Year
2024
Company
MoMo
Role
Product Owner
Team
4 people

MoMo is Vietnam's No.1 e-wallet, and its Home screen is where many services compete for one glance. I turned the badge, the small mark that says "look here", into one shared system every business unit could use.

Get the appApp Store ↗Google Play ↗

01Outcome

Business units now have one reusable way to ask for attention on the Home screen, and adding a new badge takes much less engineering work.

30%higher engagement rate at most (clicks on a service divided by its impressions, per user)
3business units grew their engaged users with it

02The problem

MoMo's Home screen is crowded. Many business units wanted people to open their mini apps, and each one struggled to stand out.

Everyone wanted to be noticed on the same small screen.

Building a separate badge for each team would have meant making the same thing again and again.

03My role

I was Product Owner with a team of 4: 1 Product Designer, 2 Software Engineers and 1 QC Engineer.

  1. Listened to many business units

    Gathered what each one needed from a badge.

  2. Turned different needs into one proposal

    One design that most teams could use.

  3. Led design and engineering

    Delivered it on time.

  4. Supported business units with operations

    Stayed with them once badges went live.

04The solution

Three calls shaped it.

  1. One system for most needs, built to scale.

    Each team expected something a little different from a badge. We made trade-offs to support most of their needs while keeping the solution scalable.

  2. Three badge types: dot, number and text.

    Each team chooses how loudly to ask, from a quiet dot to a clear message.

  3. One formal process, from request to operations.

    Teams asked for badges and needed support once they were live. One shared way to do both kept delivery on time.

Three ways to say "look here"

Plays on its own. Hover to pause, or pick a badge. Sample data.

05What I learned

On a platform, building for one team is easy. The real work is one scalable system every team can reuse, and a clear process to say yes, no or later to the rest.
